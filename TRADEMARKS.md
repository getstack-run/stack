# STACK trademark policy

Version 1.0.

The code in this repository is licensed under the licences listed in `LICENSE`. Those licences give you the code. They do not give you the names. This page says how the names may be used.

## What this covers

- the name **STACK**, as the name of this project and its software and services;
- the **STACK logo**;
- the phrase **"STACK-verified"**.

Together, "the marks". They belong to Tomi Lupsakko.

## What you may do without asking

You may use the name STACK, in plain text, to say something true about STACK or about your own work in relation to it:

- that your software **works with**, **integrates with** or **is compatible with** STACK, or with the STACK specifications in `spec/`;
- that you **run** your own installation of the STACK engine;
- that your product is **based on** or **a fork of** STACK, when it is, and that it is not the official STACK;
- in articles, documentation, talks, reviews and comparisons, as long as they are accurate.

Put your own name first and STACK second ("Acme Agents for STACK", "works with STACK"), never the other way round. Do not make the name more prominent than your own. Do not suggest that STACK made, approved, audited or endorses what you are describing.

You may link to the project's pages and repository.

## What you may not do without written permission

- Use STACK, or a name that could be mistaken for it, as the name of your product, service, company, package, repository, domain name or social media account.
- Distribute a modified version of the software under the name STACK. A fork has to carry its own name. You may say it is derived from STACK.
- Use the logo, or a logo that looks like it.
- Say or imply that STACK sponsors, certifies, audits or endorses you, your product or your agents.
- Say your software is "STACK-compatible" or "STACK-certified" because it passed a test, unless it did pass the conformance kit in `spec/conformance` for what you claim, and you say which suites.

## "STACK-verified"

"STACK-verified" is a statement that only STACK's own network may make. It means one thing: at the time of the statement, STACK countersigned this agent's passport for an installation, and STACK has not published a revocation of it. It does not mean the agent is safe, that its owner or its output can be trusted, or that STACK has reviewed what it does.

- Only the STACK network issues a statement under this name, and only through its signed "verified" statement. Nobody else may issue, imitate or sell something called "STACK-verified", or a similar name ("STACK-certified", "STACK-approved", "STACK-secured").
- Someone who checks a passport may show the words "STACK-verified" next to the agent that was checked while they hold a current statement from the STACK network for it, in plain text and not as a badge. They may not use the words about themselves, their product or their site.
- Nobody may show it for an agent whose statement has expired or been revoked.
- An installation of the engine that does not use STACK's network cannot make its own agents "STACK-verified". It may say its agents carry passports signed by its own installation.

## Forks and your own installations

Running, changing and redistributing the code under its licences is up to you. If you publish a changed version, give it another name, keep the copyright and licence notices, and say that it is derived from STACK. Do not publish it as STACK, or as an official version.

## Reporting a misuse, and changes

Write to support@getstack.run with where you saw the use. STACK may ask someone to stop a use that confuses people about who makes a product, and may stop countersigning or revoke a key used to abuse the network.

STACK may change this policy. A use that was allowed stays allowed for 30 days after a change that narrows it.
